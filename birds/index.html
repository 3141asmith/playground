READING THE SPECTROGRAM

See what your ears hear.

The horizontal axis shows time and the vertical axis shows frequency, measured in kilohertz. Bright marks are louder sounds. A pure tone makes a horizontal line; a rising whistle curves upwards. Repeated shapes can reveal a bird’s repeated phrases.

The test sound is a generated whistle and trill for exploring this display. It is not a bird recording and cannot validate a species identification.

ABOUT THE IDENTIFICATIONS

A starting point for discovery.

BirdNET compares the audio with its learned sound patterns. Wind, traffic, overlapping birds and distant calls can produce incorrect matches. Compare the timestamped suggestions with what you hear. No location or seasonal filtering is applied.

This tool needs the original audio, rather than an image of a spectrogram. It mixes channels to mono and resamples to 48 kHz for the model. The display shows frequencies up to 12 kHz; resampling cannot recover frequencies absent from the original recording.
