Reading the diagram

Main-sequence dwarfs run from hot, bright stars towards cool, faint stars. Giants and supergiants sit above them: a larger surface can radiate much more light, even at a lower temperature.

L☉ and R☉ mean the Sun’s luminosity and radius. Luminosity includes all wavelengths.

Two kinds of point. Named stellar and substellar objects come from Stellar Catalog. The 131 ESO-hosted Pickles templates represent composite spectral types, not individual stars. Objects sharing a spectral type are distinct stars; templates are kept as separate reference points. Repeated catalogue IDs and canonical names are merged, preserving separate binary components. Colours indicate spectral classes; D denotes white dwarfs, L/T/Y substellar types, and ? an unlisted type.

Where the numbers come from. ESO temperatures and indicative bolometric magnitudes come from Pickles (1998), Table 2 via CDS. L/L☉ = 10(4.74 − Mbol)/2.5; R/R☉ = √(L/L☉) × (5772 K / T)2. Named objects use catalogue values; if one of T, L or R is missing, it is derived from the other two and identified in the details. Objects without enough data are omitted.
