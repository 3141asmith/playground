About this selection

Averages, median and population standard deviation use the actual readings, with equal weight per reading. Missing periods are not filled in. A gap means more than 3× the median interval between readings.

Dates and times are shown exactly as logged, with no timezone conversion. Weeks run Monday–Sunday.

Distribution and completeness

Where is time missing?

    Percentiles describe the readings, using linear interpolation between ranked values. Time coverage compares usable intervals with the full selected period; for All data, it uses the first-to-last reading span. Expected counts assume the log's typical sampling interval.

    Daily patterns

    Daily ranges use days with at least two readings. Partial days can understate the range. Typical high/low times are circular means of each varying day's first recorded extreme; flat days are excluded. A balanced spread around the clock has no typical time.

    Warming, cooling and trends

    Rates use complete windows ending at readings, interpolating the window's starting temperature. Windows crossing long gaps are excluded. Trends fit a straight line to daily means or weekly means (each observed day weighted equally); partial days/weeks are included. These describe the selection and do not establish an annual seasonal pattern.

    Day-to-day changes

    Hover over a mark for details. The daily summary below also lists each day's observed range and extreme times.

    How duration, coverage and trend estimates work

    Temperatures are assumed to change linearly between nearby readings (trapezoidal integration for the time-weighted average). No values are inferred before the first selected reading, after the last, or across a long gap. Unknown time is excluded from threshold durations and the time-weighted average. With fewer than two usable readings these estimates are unavailable. The gap cutoff uses 3× the median interval of the complete uploaded log.

    Trend slopes are ordinary least-squares fits against actual elapsed days. R² describes the fit to the observed means, not a forecast or proof of a cause. At least two distinct days/weeks are required. Percentiles are reading-weighted, and time-weighted means use only covered intervals.
